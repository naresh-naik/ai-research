The Solar System consists of the Sun, eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, and comets. Gravity keeps these objects in stable orbits.

Stars generate energy through nuclear fusion inside their cores. Hydrogen atoms combine to form helium while releasing enormous amounts of energy.

Galaxies contain billions of stars along with gas, dust, and dark matter. The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System.

The Moon orbits the Earth approximately every twenty-seven days. Its phases result from changing angles between the Earth, Moon, and Sun.

Black holes possess gravitational fields so strong that even light cannot escape once it crosses the event horizon.

Astronomers study distant planets using telescopes that detect visible light, infrared radiation, and radio waves.

Comets develop bright tails when solar radiation heats the ice inside them.

The universe has been expanding since the Big Bang approximately thirteen point eight billion years ago.

Space telescopes avoid atmospheric distortion and produce clearer observations of distant galaxies.

The Sun will eventually expand into a red giant before ending its life as a white dwarf.
