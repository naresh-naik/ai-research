The Earth is divided into seven continents and five oceans. Each continent has its own geography, climate, and ecosystems. Mountains, rivers, deserts, and forests shape the environment and influence human civilization.

Countries are separated by political borders but connected through trade, transportation, and culture. Capitals serve as administrative centers where governments operate and major institutions are located.

Rivers often begin in mountain regions and flow toward oceans or inland lakes. Many ancient civilizations developed near rivers because they provided fresh water, fertile soil, and transportation.

Mountain ranges influence climate by blocking winds and creating rain shadows. The Himalayas, Andes, Rockies, and Alps are among the world's major mountain systems.

Latitude determines the amount of sunlight a region receives throughout the year. Tropical regions remain warm while polar regions experience long winters and short summers.

Volcanoes form where tectonic plates interact. Some volcanoes remain dormant for centuries before erupting, while others remain active for long periods.

The Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean on Earth and contains thousands of islands. Ocean currents influence weather patterns and global climate.

Deserts receive very little rainfall, but they support specialized plants and animals adapted to harsh conditions.

Forests absorb carbon dioxide and release oxygen through photosynthesis. They provide habitats for millions of species.

Maps use symbols, colors, and scales to represent physical and political features of the Earth.
